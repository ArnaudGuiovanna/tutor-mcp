1.0 Learner state →

The missing layer is not content. It is continuity.

Your AI handles the conversation. Tutor MCP gives it two persistent layers, so the next session starts from what you have mastered, forgotten, misunderstood and committed to do next.

The loop is simple: choose an activity, teach and practice, record the response, update the learning state. The client calls get_next_activity for guidance and record_interaction to save observations.

structured state

Learning engine

Concepts, prerequisites, mastery estimates, review timing and assessment evidence. Chooses what to practice, revisit or assess next.

narrative memory

Session memory

Session summaries, goals, recurring misconceptions and useful learner context, encrypted and versioned. The AI picks up the thread.

assessment

Frozen evidence

Tasks and rubrics are frozen before the response. Estimated, retained, demonstrated and transferred skills are kept apart.

audit

Decision trail

Decision snapshots and replay explain why an activity was recommended or a concept held back.

Concept states

Mastered

Above the routing threshold, with assessment evidence.

In progress

Unlocked, BKT estimate moving.

Review due

FSRS predicts retention below target.

Locked

Prerequisites not met yet. Never red.

2.0 Use cases →

Six ways to use your tutor.

No catalogue is required. Tell your AI what you want to learn; the engine handles sequencing, pacing and reviews, and says when you have actually got it. Institutions can add a shared catalogue on top.

“I want to learn Spanish before my trip in six weeks.”

Pick up something new

State a goal and a deadline. The engine maps the path and only opens new ground when the basics are solid.

languages · coding · a new craft

A short diagnostic shows what is still solid, what faded and where the small wrong ideas hide. You revisit only what needs it.

Drills focus on weak spots; reviews are scheduled so retention peaks close to the date.

3.0 Tutor ACP →

One desktop client to learn and manage.

Tutor ACP connects Codex or Claude to your institution’s learning service over the Agent Client Protocol. Members find shared programmes in one catalogue, learn at their own pace and come back to their conversations and progress.

Conversation and Formations bring together tutoring, the programme library, manual or AI-assisted editing and progress. Account and Settings cover agents, institution, members and reading preferences. French and English, light, dark and system themes.

Download for Windows x64Client guide

Bundled agents

Codex and Claude adapters and their Node.js runtime are included. Connect your provider account; it stays personal.

Public-key identities

Your identity and recovery key are generated locally and backed up encrypted. The server stores public keys only; the agent never sees a private key.

Explicit permissions

MCP tool permissions can be remembered per account, server and agent, and changed in Settings. Interrupted answers stay marked as such.

4.0 Architecture and algorithms →

The AI explains. Tutor MCP remembers and decides.

The runtime owns the durable learner state and the pedagogical decisions. The model stays free to explain, reframe, question and summarize. Decisions are inspectable; their quality depends on the evidence the AI records.

5.0 Profiles →

Your learning engine, where you want it.

Same binaries, three profiles. Every profile uses the same learning engine and the same MCP tools. Connect the clients you use to the same installation to keep one learning history.

tutor-mcp --local

Local

Learning on your own computer. Your MCP client starts the binary over stdio; SQLite stores your history in ~/.tutor-mcp/local. No account or server setup.

tutor-mcp --profile hobby

Hobby

A personal VPS or a small group using MCP clients. SQLite and an HTTPS proxy. Invite users over SSH; they sign in with a username and password.

tutor-mcp --profile institution

Institution

Learning communities, teams and training organizations. A shared catalogue, member access, contribution review. Public-key identities, PostgreSQL and separate API, worker and migrator roles.

8.0 FAQ →

Questions you might have.

Short answers. The documentation and the repository have the details.

Do I need an account with Anthropic, OpenAI, Mistral or Google?

Tutor MCP does not ship a language model. It plugs into the assistant you already use: Claude Code, Claude Desktop, ChatGPT, Hermes, Pi, Gemini CLI, Le Chat and other MCP clients. Tutor ACP bundles the Codex and Claude adapters; your provider account and its costs stay separate from Tutor. The conversation lives in that client; the learning state lives in your Tutor installation.

Where does my learning data live?

In the Tutor installation you choose. Local: a private SQLite directory on your machine, ~/.tutor-mcp/local. Hobby: SQLite on your own VPS behind HTTPS. Institution: PostgreSQL with tenant isolation and operator-managed keys. Narrative memory is encrypted and versioned in the database. There is no telemetry.

Can I learn with several AI clients and keep one history?

Yes. Connect them to the same Tutor installation, locally over stdio or remotely over HTTPS with OAuth. Multiple local clients can share one profile and its learner identity. Separate installations stay independent; nothing synchronizes implicitly.

How is this different from flashcards, a language app or a prompt around a chatbot?

A flashcard app schedules reviews. A language app plays a fixed item bank. A prompt around a chatbot forgets. Tutor MCP is the layer between them: a persistent, auditable engine that tracks estimates, retention, demonstrated skills, transfer, misconceptions and narrative memory, and decides what comes next. The AI creates and explains.

Is this production-ready?

Tutor MCP is in alpha. The engine, the three profiles and the Windows client are tested in CI, and only the latest release tag and main receive fixes. Software tests establish implementation behaviour, not measured learning effectiveness; estimates are never presented as proof of mastery.

Can I host this for a classroom, a team or a training organization?

Yes. The institution profile runs on PostgreSQL with separate API, worker and migrator roles. Members use public-key identities and the Tutor ACP client; owners manage membership and access; authors and authorised trainers maintain programmes. The hobby profile fits a small group on one VPS.

Why Go?

One static binary per platform, pure-Go SQLite without CGO, low memory and concurrency for parallel learner loops. Binaries target Linux, macOS and Windows on amd64 and arm64; building from source needs Go 1.26.10 or a newer patched release.
