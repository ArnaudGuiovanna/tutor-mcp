3.0 Share what you build →

Your next course could help someone else start.

Build something for your own learning, then contribute its design to your institution. A shared course can evolve without turning your private learning history into public content.

Follow the course-sharing guide
  1. 01

    Create a private course

    Start with a question or a learning goal. Build and refine the programme in your own space.

  2. 02

    Share its design

    Create a copy for your institution. Your conversations, observations and personal progress stay out of that copy.

  3. 03

    Review, then publish

    The owner examines contributions before publication. Offer the approved version in the catalogue or invite named members.

  4. 04

    Let people make it their own

    Members choose to enrol and learn at their own pace. Their enrolled version stays stable as the course evolves.

The course design is shared. Your learning history is not. Conversations, private observations and personal progress are not copied when you share a course.

5.0 Tutor ACP →

Your conversations and courses, in one place.

Conversation is where learning starts. Courses brings together your private programmes and the courses available to you in your institution. Refine a programme manually or examine an AI proposal in the same editor.

Account and Settings cover agents, institution, members and reading preferences. The client supports French and English, light, dark and system themes, text size and reduced motion.

Download Tutor ACPSet up the Windows client

Codex and Claude included

The adapters and their runtime are bundled. Choose an agent and connect your provider account, without a separate Node.js installation.

Your identity, created locally

For an institution, create or import your public-key identity and save its encrypted backup. Keep that backup so you can recover your identity.

Permissions you can revisit

Allow, ask or refuse MCP tools for the selected account, server and agent. Change those choices in Settings; they never grant extra institution roles.

6.0 Choose where to run Tutor →

Start on your computer.
Grow into a community.

The same server supports three deployment profiles. Start with the route that fits you; the guides separate everyday learning from server administration.

Find your installation path
tutor-mcp --local

Local

Your MCP client starts the server on your computer. SQLite stores your learning state; no Tutor account or hosted server is required.

Start locally
tutor-mcp --profile hobby

Hobby

Run a personal server or a small group on a VPS, with SQLite and HTTPS. Members of this profile sign in with a username and password.

See the Hobby profile
tutor-mcp --profile institution

Institution

A shared catalogue, public-key identities, members and reviewed contributions. PostgreSQL and separate service roles support the institution deployment.

Deploy for a community

Local learning state does not mean a local AI model. Your selected provider processes the content sent to it. Use the client and provider configuration that fits your needs.

7.0 Inside the learning engine →

The AI explains.
Tutor MCP remembers and plans.

The model creates explanations, exercises and feedback. The engine maintains the learning state and selects activities from the recorded evidence. Its decisions are inspectable; their quality depends on the observations provided.

Read the algorithm guide
BKT · evidence gates

Estimate and demonstrate

Knowledge tracing updates estimates from responses. Assessment evidence is tracked separately, so a high probability alone does not certify mastery.

FSRS-5 · prerequisites

Review and progress

Spaced review estimates retention. A prerequisite graph helps determine which concepts are accessible and what should be revisited.

Session memory · decision replay

Remember and inspect

Versioned, encrypted narrative memory carries useful session context. Decision snapshots and replay help explain the recommended next step.

These are software capabilities, not a claim of measured learning effectiveness. Tutor is alpha, and its documentation describes the implementation and its limits.

9.0 FAQ →

A few things before you start.

Choose your installation, connect your AI provider and start with one learning goal. The guides walk through each step.

What do I need to start?

On Windows x64, install Tutor ACP, connect Codex or Claude with your own provider account, then connect Tutor in local mode or join your institution. Tutor ACP includes the agent adapters and their runtime; you do not need to install Node.js separately. Local mode also requires the separate Tutor MCP server binary. If you already use an MCP client, you can install only the Tutor MCP server.

Are Tutor MCP and Tutor ACP the same thing?

They work together. Tutor MCP is the server that stores courses, learning state and session memory and provides the pedagogical tools. Tutor ACP is the desktop application for conversations, courses and institution settings. You can also connect another compatible MCP client to Tutor MCP.

Is everything free?

Both projects are open source under the MIT licence. You can use and self-host them without a Tutor subscription. Your chosen AI provider may charge for its service, and hosting an institution has infrastructure costs. Tutor does not include a language model.

Where does my data live?

The learning state lives in the Tutor installation you choose: SQLite on your computer for local use, SQLite on your VPS for Hobby, or PostgreSQL for an institution. Tutor ACP stores conversations on your device. Your AI provider processes the content sent to it under its own terms; a local Tutor server does not make a remote AI model local.

What happens when I share a course?

Tutor copies the course design, not your private conversations, observations or progress. Contributions follow the owner’s review workflow. An institution can offer a published version in its catalogue or invite named members. Joining remains voluntary, and enrolled learners keep their course version.

Can I use several AI clients and keep my progress?

Yes, when they connect to the same Tutor installation and learner identity. Separate installations stay independent; nothing synchronizes implicitly. The connection guides explain which local and remote configurations each client supports.

Is Tutor ready for everyone?

Tutor is alpha software. The repository documents its tests, supported configurations and known limits. Software tests establish implementation behaviour, not measured learning effectiveness. Mastery estimates are kept distinct from demonstrated skills.

Can I modify it or use it commercially?

Yes. The MIT licence permits use, modification and redistribution, including commercially, while preserving its copyright and licence notices. It is provided as is, without warranty. The source and contribution guides are on GitHub.
